transit durations of the known planets L 98-59 d and TOI-620 b (sections [6.6](6.6_L98-59d.md), [6.7](6.7_TOI-620b.md)); an ephemeris update of TOI-6482.01 (section [6.8](6.8_TOI-6482.md)); an increasing transit depth of the planet candidate TOI-2638.01 (section [6.9](6.9_TOI-2638.md)); a transit-like signal at the period and phase of the radial-velocity planet TOI-1117 c (section [6.10](6.10_TOI-1117c.md)).

Three further results (section [6](6_other_results.md)). TIC 11613676 (CD-30 448, K dwarf, $`T = 10.50`$) is an eclipsing binary with
$`P = 12.1158`$ d whose eclipses were absent in TESS sector 3 (2018; depth $`< 0.04`$ %), present in sector 30 (2020;
0.69 % and 0.53 %), 5.4 % and 2.2 % deep in sector 97 (2025) and shallower in sectors 104 and 106 (2026; 4.0 % and
1.7 % in sector 106); the eclipse times deviate from a linear ephemeris by up to 30 min, Gaia DR3 gives RUWE 48 and a
radial-velocity amplitude of 33 km/s, and the geometric model adopted here has the orbital inclination oscillating
with a period of about 23 yr, as nodal precession by a third body would produce; the
star has no variable-star or eclipsing-binary entry in the catalogues checked. TOI-540 b (Ment et al. 2021), a known
planet, transits in TESS sectors 4-32 (2018-2020) at about 1.7 ppt; in TESS sector 98 (2025-11 to 2026-01) the transits
are not detected in any product examined ($`81 \pm 268`$ ppm). The checks find no instrumental cause; ground-based
photometry of one transit (windows in section [6.2.9](6.2_TOI-540b.md#629-decisive-test)) would decide between a data problem and a change of the transit
geometry. TIC 124751008 (2MASS J06162084-3509125, an M-dwarf multiple at 57 pc) is a 12.61-d eclipsing binary whose
primary eclipses, measured in the same aperture, were 2.25 % deep in 2018, 0.28 % in TESS sector 87 (2024-2025) and
0.77 % in sector 109 (2026); the 0.86 % secondary eclipse of 2019 has not been detected since 2021, and the eclipse times
change by up to 43 min between consecutive cycles. The geometric models require a change of the orbital inclination
with a minimum near 2024.3 (a sinusoid gives a period of about 20 yr), as nodal precession by a third body would
produce; the star has no variable-star or eclipsing-binary entry in the catalogues checked (section [6.4](6.4_TIC124751008.md)).

Two known planets are candidates for a decreasing transit duration. For L 98-59 d (TOI-175.02, $`b \approx 0.92`$) a joint
fit of 72 TESS transits (2018.65-2026.01; campaign mean epochs 2019.13-2025.44) gives evidence for a secular decrease
of the transit duration, $`\mathrm{d}T_{14}/\mathrm{d}t = -1.185 \pm 0.112`$ min/yr (about 51 to 44 min; nominal local
$`z`$ = 6.7-7.4; an illustrative Gaussian trials adjustment gives $`z`$ = 5.4-6.4, subject to the assumptions in section
6.6.10), with no significant improvement from allowing the radius ratio to vary between campaigns; the evidence rests
on four observing campaigns (1.8 $`\sigma`$ within campaigns), and planets b and c show no change in the same data. The
two JWST NIRSpec detectors differ by about 0.8 min in each visit, with opposite signs. With the post-hoc detector
averaging and empirical systematic floor, the NIRSpec pair differs from no change by 1.3 $`\sigma`$. Adding NIRISS gives
a slope 2.2 $`\sigma`$ from zero. Both are consistent with the TESS trend; JWST does not independently confirm it. An
earlier search by Bessho et al. (2025) found a change of the same sign that was not significant
(section [6.6](6.6_L98-59d.md)). TOI-620 b is a weaker candidate: its
transit duration falls from about 80 min (2019) to about 62 min (2025-2026) in five TESS sectors
($`-2.9 \pm 0.4`$ min/yr, $`p \approx 0.006`$ with three degrees of freedom; the slope depends on the aperture), and its
transit times vary by up to about $`\pm 7`$ min between sectors, consistent in amplitude with the earlier TTV report by Naponiello (2026; section [6.7](6.7_TOI-620b.md)).

For the known planet candidate TOI-6482.01 (TIC 189300053), listed in the TOI table with $`P = 1483.5175`$ d, six TESS
QLP transits (sectors 8-99, 2019-2026) give $`P = 20.047937 \pm 0.000060`$ d (the TOI-table period is close to 74 times this
value; TFOP notes on ExoFOP give 20.04753 d, the TOI-table period divided by 74), a depth of about 9.4 ppt and $`R_\mathrm{p} \approx 17\,R_\oplus`$ with the
TIC stellar radius; the nature of the transiting object is not established; an earlier version of this ephemeris was
uploaded to ExoFOP on 2026-10-20 (section [6.8](6.8_TOI-6482.md)).

The transit of the TESS planet candidate TOI-2638.01 (TIC 458342769, $`P = 3.93`$ d; an A-type star with Gaia DR3
$`T_\mathrm{eff} \approx 8800`$-9500 K and $`v \sin i = 110 \pm 7`$ km/s) deepens from 1.8 ppt in 2021 to 4.3 ppt in 2026 in the
SPOC 2-min PDCSAP light curves (1.6 ppt in the 2019 QLP light curve), $`+384 \pm 56`$ ppm/yr (5.9 $`\sigma`$ in a bootstrap
over sectors), in every TESS product examined and in own photometry of the target pixel files, with constant implied
target flux and the signal located on the target rather than on a 17" neighbour. Its duration measured with a free
depth lengthens from about 60 to about 100 min, but the shape change that separates a change of the transit geometry
from a change of dilution is only about 2.6-3.1 $`\sigma`$. A grazing companion whose impact parameter decreases from
about 1.00 to 0.95, for example by nodal precession driven by the oblateness of the rapidly rotating star, describes
the data; the cause and the nature of the companion are not established. TESS sectors 111-113 (2026-11 to 2027-02)
test the extrapolation (about 4.2 ppt and 106 min in the linear trends). Only TESS data are used (section [6.9](6.9_TOI-2638.md)).

TOI-1117 (TIC 295541511) has a transiting sub-Neptune b and two planets, c and d, known from HARPS radial velocities and
described as non-transiting (Lockley et al. 2025). TESS sectors 13, 39 and 100-103 show a transit-like signal at
$`P = 4.5786510^{+0.0000087}_{-0.0000085}`$ d, about 490 ppm deep and 1.85 h long (BLS S/N 12.4; 27 transits, all with
positive depth), whose ephemeris agrees with the RV orbit of c within 1.0 $`\sigma`$ at every epoch and in period
(0.16 $`\sigma`$). If it is the transit of c, $`R_\mathrm{p} = 2.73^{+0.16}_{-0.15}\,R_\oplus`$ and, with
$`K_c = 3.47 \pm 0.45`$ m/s from the published velocities at the transit ephemeris, $`M_\mathrm{p} = 8.9 \pm 1.2\,M_\oplus`$.
The signal is not validated: a $`G = 19.6`$ star at 34" is disfavoured as its source only at $`\Delta\chi^2 = 7.5`$ in the
pixel data, the shape with the stellar-density prior needs $`b = 0.85`$ while the fit without it (with uniform limb-darkening
parameters) favours a boxier transit ($`\chi^2`$ lower by 3.5, not calibrated as a statistical significance), and the
TRICERATOPS false-positive probabilities do not reach the validation thresholds (with an approximate contrast curve FPP
0.0146-0.0149, just below 0.015, but NFPP 0.013, above 0.001). Only TESS data and the published velocities are used (section [6.10](6.10_TOI-1117c.md)).

LHS 475 b (TIC 369327947, GJ 4102), an Earth-sized planet on a 2.03-d orbit whose transmission spectrum was measured
with JWST, shows a candidate decrease of its transit duration in ten TESS sectors: from $`42.6 \pm 0.9`$ min in 2019-2021
to $`35.4 \pm 0.9`$ min in 2025-2026, with the transit shape fixed to the published fit. At $`a/R_\star = 15.77`$ the impact
parameter rises at $`+0.0156 \pm 0.0043`$ yr$`^{-1}`$ with per-sector errors calibrated by injection-recovery (local
3.7 $`\sigma`$; 3.0-4.4 $`\sigma`$ for other calibrations; $`+0.0185 \pm 0.0045`$ yr$`^{-1}`$ with the fit errors). The planet
was selected from a screen of 271 known planets, which leaves about 2-3 $`\sigma`$; the rate in $`b`$ depends on the adopted
$`a/R_\star`$; the change appears in SPOC, QLP and own photometry, which share the same photons. An own refit of the two
public JWST transits of 2022 gives $`T_{14} = 39.85 \pm 0.10`$ (stat) $`\pm 0.2`$ (sys) min, the value of the TESS trend at that
epoch, 3.1 $`\sigma`$ from a constant at the 2019-2021 value, but consistent with a constant near the TESS mean, so it does not
confirm the change. The linear trend gives $`T_{14} \approx 34`$ min in late 2026 against 39-43 min for the constant
alternatives; TESS sector 114 (about 2027-02/03) and precise ground-based or JWST transits test it (section [6.11](6.11_LHS475b.md)).
